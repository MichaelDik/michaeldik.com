Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World

by Cal Newport
★★★★★ 5 of 5 stars

Wonderful book that came at the right time in my life. Another review described it as one of those things that you probably know but reading really helps you know. Have wanted to pick this one up for a while.

The core idea is that while deep work—defined as "activities performed in a state of distraction-free concentration that push your cognitive capabilities to their limit"—has become increasingly rare, it has also become increasingly more valuable.

The book details the value of deep work then strategies to engage in it. Key takeaways are finding 3-4 hours in the day in separate sessions to put away distractions (phone, email, slack, social media, etc.). In these sessions you can re-build the muscle to concentrate deeply on hard tasks like computer programming, reading, writing, etc. and can create enormous output if done correctly and consistently.

It is also a skill and the point is to push through once it becomes hard (sort of reminds me of adding on lots of volume with marathon running). Final note is that focused work is a more meaningful life and more rewarding than scattered shallow work.

My take: I look forward to being a worse texter. 📵

So Good They Can't Ignore You: Why Skills Trump Passion in the Quest for Work You Love

by Cal Newport
★★★★★ 5 of 5 stars

"Following your passion is what can the world can offer me? But the more compelling and purposeful career comes from asking what can I offer the world?" [paraphrased, some words my own]

I definitely liked this, core idea is that following your passion is bad advice for most people. He argues that you need to get good at something before you will enjoy it, need to acquire skills (what he calls 'career capital') and approach work with a 'craftsman' mindset.

I took this as a mindset of approaching work for the process of getting better, developing skills and competence (a rewarding process,) and then once you get good, passion will follow. Who doesn't like being good at something? I have seen this cycle play out a bit in my life, getting good at something, then liking it more, which causes a feedback loop of wanting to get better, so the book's thesis resonated with me. He offers various implementation strategies for getting better and exploring options.

Also interesting how this was a precursor to Deep Work and you can watch as he develops his ideas that he lays out in Deep Work. Big thing was this idea of 'deliberate practice', the idea that to acquire skills that are above average and can lead to a meaningful career, you have to deliberately grow and stretch your abilities—direct predecessor to Deep Work.

The Singularity Is Nearer: When We Merge with AI

by Ray Kurzweil
★★★★☆ 4 of 5 stars

Inspired to read by Sam Altman's blog post on the gentle singularity.

TLDR: We are past the point where AI can help make AI smarter which will accelerate growth, leading to superintelligence in the late 2020s. As AI becomes smarter and smarter we can apply it to multiple other areas of life (healthcare, transportation, jobs) and we will eventually merge our brains with the AI via neural implants like Neuralink and layer on an incredible amount of intelligence. We also have to mitigate AI existential risks, job loss, political structures, etc.

Review: Definitely interesting. If AI compounds and hits exponential takeoff through recursive development (superintelligent AI creating and improving its code and intelligence faster than humans could—basically at the speed of light and electrons) then this all seems plausible.

Near term: continuing to be a user and builder of AI tools seems like the move. Long term: probably bullish on economic growth barring catastrophe (but I am optimistic, we have not nuked ourselves yet!).

Not sure reading the whole book is necessary, but definitely interesting and a good framework for how the future might unfold. I enjoy Kurzweil putting dates for when he thinks certain technologies will come into existence—it's incredibly bold but fun for a book like this to do. I look forward to tracking progress.
